# Michelin's New Tires Could Boost EV Range by 10%

Michelin's new Primacy 5 Energy line is built around a simple premise: a meaningful slice of EV efficiency is lost at the road surface, and it's possible to claw it back without compromising grip or longevity.

The headline number is up to a **10% range improvement** versus comparable touring tires.

## How they get there

The mechanism is rolling resistance. Three contributors matter:

1. **Tread compound**: lower-hysteresis silica blends that flex with less heat loss
2. **Casing geometry**: stiffer sidewalls reduce squirm under load
3. **Tread pattern**: optimized void volume for water clearance without unnecessary contact-patch deformation

Each contributor is a few percent on its own; combined, they add up to the headline number on EPA-style cycle tests.

## Why this is a smart engineering bet

Bigger batteries are expensive, heavy, and still constrained by raw materials. *Reducing the energy required per mile* is a parallel path that scales differently:

- It applies to every existing vehicle on the road, retroactively, at the next tire change.
- It improves charging economics — fewer kWh per mile means fewer trips to a charger and lower per-mile fuel cost.
- It compounds with other efficiency gains (aero, regen tuning, route optimization).

A 10% range gain at the tire is roughly equivalent to a 10% larger battery — without the weight, cost, or supply-chain consequences.

## What about wear and grip?

The trade-off used to be ugly: efficiency tires meant noisy, harder-wearing, lower-grip rubber. The Primacy 5 Energy line claims grip parity with the prior Primacy generation and treadwear ratings within the touring-tire range. Independent testing through model-year 2026 will be the real verdict; early reviewer impressions have been positive.

## What it means for fleet operators

Tire choice is rarely the first thing a fleet manager optimizes — but at fleet scale, a 10% range gain compounds with utilization patterns to materially reduce the number of charging events per shift. That's the metric that actually shapes whether your charging infrastructure stays sized for current demand or gets pushed past capacity by growth.
