# From Gas Pain to Electric Gain: A Shift in Driving

Geopolitical conflict has a way of showing up at the gas station before it shows up anywhere else in a household budget. The latest cycle of disruption — driven by tensions in the Middle East and a tightening global supply outlook — has done what every shock before it has: it's exposed the gap between drivers who pay variable energy prices and drivers who don't.

## The volatility gap

EV owners aren't immune to energy markets, but they're insulated in ways gas drivers aren't:

- **Electricity prices move slowly.** Utility rate cases take months; gas-pump prices move overnight.
- **Time-of-use programs reward off-peak charging.** Most EV households charge between midnight and 6 a.m. at residential off-peak rates that have stayed remarkably stable.
- **Solar and storage smooth the curve further.** Charging infrastructure paired with on-site solar is no longer exotic — it's a standard configuration on new commercial deployments.

When gas crosses $5/gal, an EV owner's per-mile cost barely flinches. That's not a marketing claim — it's what utility bills actually do.

## Why the U.S. has lagged anyway

Despite the obvious math, U.S. EV adoption has trailed Europe and China. The reasons are familiar:

1. Policy whiplash between administrations has chilled long-term planning.
2. Misinformation about range, fires, and grid capacity has outpaced corrections.
3. Charging infrastructure rolled out unevenly, leaving real and perceived gaps.

The current price-shock cycle is the first one happening alongside a mature charging buildout — NEVI corridors, multifamily Level 2, and DC fast at retail are all far better deployed than they were in 2022. The combination of pump pain and finally-available infrastructure is the closest thing to a true inflection point we've had.

## What it means for site hosts

Every gas-price shock is also a leasing event for properties that already offer charging. Tenants and visitors who were "thinking about an EV someday" become "actively shopping" overnight, and your charging amenity stops being a nice-to-have — it becomes the reason their rental decision tipped one direction.

The hosts who didn't install charging because "EV adoption is slow" tend to discover, during these cycles, that adoption was never the constraint. Access was.
