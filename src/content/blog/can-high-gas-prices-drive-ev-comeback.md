# Can High Gas Prices Drive an EV Comeback?

Rising gas prices, driven by global tensions, could push more people to consider electric vehicles — but the immediate sales response is rarely as dramatic as the cable-news graphics imply.

## Why the conversion isn't instant

EVs are cheaper to run than gas cars, often by a wide margin once you account for fuel and maintenance differences. The friction lives elsewhere:

- **Sticker shock.** New EVs still cost more up front than the gas equivalent, even after credits.
- **Charging access.** "Where will I plug it in?" remains the question that stalls otherwise-ready buyers, especially renters and multi-family residents.
- **Resale uncertainty.** Battery health is still a wildcard most consumers haven't priced.

Even when interest spikes alongside fuel costs, many shoppers treat the spike as temporary — and wait it out at the pump rather than at the dealer.

## What actually moves the needle

The signal worth watching isn't new-EV registrations during a price shock; it's the **used-EV market** and the **sub-$30K segment**.

- Used-EV listings have been moving faster on every spike since 2023, and trade-in valuations have firmed up as battery telemetry becomes part of dealer disclosures.
- Sub-$30K models — including the LFP-pack compacts arriving from China and Korea — are starting to undercut the gas comparison even before fuel-cost arithmetic.

Those two segments are where a sustained gas-price era would actually convert intent into ownership.

## Implications for property owners

If you're a property owner waiting on a "clear signal" before installing charging, the signal is already here — it's just slower than a headline. The pool of EV-curious tenants and visitors expands every quarter regardless of pump prices; sustained high gas just accelerates the conversion of that pool into actual EV drivers who need a place to plug in.

The properties that win this cycle are the ones that don't try to time it.
